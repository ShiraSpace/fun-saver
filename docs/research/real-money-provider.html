Real money for kids: provider and legal findings

Older kids get a real spending wallet on their phone, while savings and good deeds stay virtual. This works only through a licensed provider with an API. One provider's own docs prove the basics; three questions still block it.

Researched 2026-09-24Israel · ILSFull record: docs/research/real-money-provider.md

Verdict

Best candidate

Airwallex

Docs list an Israel consumer debit program billed in ILS, with per-card limits, merchant blocks, ATM blocking, named cards for Apple and Google Pay, and an instant sandbox.

Blocked by: licence aimed at businesses, consumer program needs approval, no documented minimum age.

Runner-up

Rapyd

Wallet and card API, free sandbox, Israeli licence. Docs don't say whether it issues cards in Israel, and its only published age rule is 18+.

The parent would have to be a "company" wallet.

Closed

Israeli kids cards

PayBox Young, Poalim Junior, MyCal, MyMax and Isracard's kids card are the right product, but none has an API.

Only route: a partnership deal.

Ruled out

Global platforms

Stripe, Adyen, Marqeta, Checkout.com, Swan, Wallester and Weavr don't issue cards to Israeli residents.

The plan

Only the spending wallet becomes real money

The parent is the customer and holds the account; child accounts sit under them, as in the app today. The kid can't touch savings, and good deeds aren't spent alone, so those two stay virtual.

Savings40%

Virtual. Parent holds the money. Interest is a labelled simulation.

Spending50%

Real money at the provider, on a card in the child's name in Apple or Google Pay.

Good deeds10%

Virtual. Parent holds the money and gives with the child.

  1. In-app weekly allowance

    Parent sets amount and weekday; the deposit posts itself with the usual split, reusing the once-per-day settlement pattern. No provider needed.

  2. Provider due diligence

    Send the questions below to Airwallex and Rapyd sales, and book a fintech lawyer.

  3. Sandbox prototype

    Airwallex sandbox: parent wallet, child cardholder, weekly transfer, balance read, card controls.

  4. Integration

    Only once a provider confirms consumer and minor support and the lawyer confirms our role.

Fallback if no provider accepts: fun-saver stays a ledger, the parent loads PayBox Young or MyCal by hand, and a "sync balance" action records the difference as spending.

Parent holds, child uses

It's legal, and the parent carries the risk

Holding a child's balance needs an Israel Securities Authority licence, so the provider holds the money and fun-saver only calls its API. The parent-held model is how every Israeli kids card already works.

Requirements from the findings

What fun-saver must build

  • A card in the child's name under the parent's contract; never the parent's card or login
  • A weekly allowance the parent approves once and cancels with one tap
  • Freeze and report-lost actions that reach the provider immediately
  • Blocks on instalments, ATM, gambling, alcohol and tobacco, crypto, money transfers and fuel
  • A balance cap on the child's wallet
  • The child's details for the provider's beneficiary record
  • Parent's informed privacy consent and a child-friendly notice
  • No marketing aimed at the child; interest labelled as a simulation
