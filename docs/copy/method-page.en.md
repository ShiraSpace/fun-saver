## Giving him practice at choosing

`key: method.goal.body` · `text`
The goal isn't that he saves. The goal is that he gets **weekly practice at
delaying gratification** — choosing between "now" and "later" — and that he has
a real reason to believe "later" actually arrives.

`key: method.goal.outcome.spending`
🛍️ **Feeling a limit** — money that runs out, and is gone.

`key: method.goal.outcome.savings`
🐷 **Practising the wait — and seeing it pay off.** Money left in savings grows
on its own.

`key: method.goal.outcome.savings.note`
That's how the first understanding of interest arrives: not from an
explanation, but from seeing a few more coins every day.

`key: method.goal.outcome.goodDeeds`
💛 **Discovering that giving feels good** — with money that's his.

`key: method.goal.derived`
And along the way he'll learn **money sense, saving and interest**. "No" stops
being the end of the story: things we won't buy him, he can buy himself.

## 1 · Why not one balance

`key: method.why.title`
### Why not one balance

`key: method.why.body` · `text`
When all the money sits in a single number, there's nothing to learn from it.
The child sees an amount, spends from it, and sees a smaller amount. There's no
moment where they have to choose.

Split that same money into three separate jars and every shekel gets a job —
and the act of separating it, on its own, changes behaviour.

`key: method.why.evidence` · `quote`
> In a field study in India, day labourers were given the same savings amount —
> some in one envelope, some split across two sealed envelopes. The ones who
> got two envelopes **saved 72% more**. Same money, same people. Only the
> partition changed.
> — *Soman & Cheema, Journal of Marketing Research, 2011*

`key: method.why.noTransfers` · `text`
That isn't a metaphor. It's why the app has **no way to move money between
jars** — a jar that's easy to open stops being a jar.

`key: method.why.honest` · `text`
Worth saying plainly: no study has tested "the three-jar method" on children
directly. What has been tested is the mechanism underneath it — separating and
earmarking money. That's what we're leaning on, and not a bigger promise.

## 2 · The three jars

`key: method.wallets.title`
### The three jars

`key: method.wallets.spending` · `text`
**🛍️ Spending — money for now.** A snack, stickers, something small at the
kiosk. He can spend it without asking, and it's the money that runs out.
**The lesson lives in the moment it runs out** — which is why it doesn't get
topped up early.

`key: method.wallets.savings` · `text`
**🐷 Savings — money for later.** Builds toward something bigger the child
chose. This is where the waiting happens, and where a few more coins appear
every day. Once there's a goal, savings stops being "money I'm not allowed to
touch" and becomes "how much further to the scooter."

`key: method.wallets.goodDeeds` · `text`
**💛 Good deeds — money for someone else.** A donation, a gift for a friend,
something for an animal, or giving to a homeless person he passes. Some
families call this a tithe — a tenth.

`key: method.wallets.goodDeeds.concrete` · `text`
At this age it's the most concrete giving that works best: someone he can see,
and money that leaves his own hand.

`key: method.wallets.goodDeeds.evidence` · `quote`
> Giving feels good, and earlier than you'd expect: toddlers **under the age of
> two** showed more happiness giving a treat away than receiving one themselves
> — and most of all when it came out of their own share.
> — *Aknin, Hamlin & Dunn, PLoS ONE, 2012*

`key: method.wallets.goodDeeds.talk` · `talk`
🗣️ **What to say**
"You've got ₪18 in your good-deeds jar. Who would you like to give it to?"
*Let the child pick who. Giving works when it's theirs — not when it's an
instruction.*

## 3 · The promise

`key: method.promise.title`
### The promise · *most important*

`key: method.promise.body` · `text`
A child waits for tomorrow's money only if they believe tomorrow's money will
actually arrive. If it comes sometimes, or gets taken away when tempers go —
the child learns that the sensible thing is to spend everything now. **And
they're right.**

`key: method.promise.evidence` · `quote`
> In a well-known experiment, before the famous waiting task, children met an
> adult who promised them something — and either kept the promise or broke it.
> The children who met an adult who broke the promise **gave up on waiting
> almost immediately**. What that measured wasn't the child's patience. It was
> the adult's reliability.
> — *Kidd, Palmeri & Aslin, Cognition, 2013*

`key: method.promise.rule.day`
📅 Allowance arrives on a **fixed day**, without having to be asked. Not as a
reward, not when you remember.

`key: method.promise.rule.noPunish`
🚫 **Money is never taken out of the jars as punishment. Ever.** If you need a
boundary, the boundary isn't the money.

`key: method.promise.action.label`
Decide

`key: method.promise.action.day`
☐ **Which day does allowance arrive?**
*Not set yet · which day doesn't matter, that it's fixed matters enormously*

## 4 · What to do

`key: method.actions.title`
### What to do · *6*

`key: method.actions.intro` · `text`
Five decisions and one conversation. Better to make the decisions before the
conversation than halfway through it.

`key: method.actions.amount` · `text`
**How much?** Israel has no official figure. At 5–7 the common range is ₪5–15
a week; at 8–10, ₪10–30. **The amount matters less than its consistency** —
it's the one thing every source agrees on.

`key: method.actions.frequency` · `text`
**How often?** Weekly until about age 9. A week is roughly as far ahead as a
7-year-old can plan. Monthly money is gone in week one and leaves three empty
weeks with no lesson in them.

`key: method.actions.split` · `text`
**How do you split it?** The default is 50/40/10. **No split has ever been
tested against another** — this is a family values decision, not a finding.

`key: method.actions.chores` · `text`
**Is it tied to chores?** No, and we'd recommend against it.

`key: method.actions.chores.evidence` · `quote`
> Ten day-care centres in Haifa introduced a fine for picking children up late,
> and lateness **went up**. When you attach money to a social obligation, the
> money replaces it — and when you remove the money, the obligation doesn't
> come back.
> — *Gneezy & Rustichini, A Fine is a Price, 2000*

`key: method.actions.rescue` · `text`
**And when the money runs out mid-week — you wait for next week.** This isn't a
decision, it's the rule: no early top-ups, and nothing taken from savings. Top
it up and there's no consequence; with no consequence there's nothing to learn,
and the three jars become decoration.

`key: method.actions.decide.label`
Decide · set in the app

`key: method.actions.decide.items`
☑ **How much per week?** — yours: **₪30**
☑ **How often?** — yours: **weekly**
☑ **How do you split it?** — in the app: **50 / 40 / 10**
☑ **Tied to chores?** — yours: **no**
☐ **What's the savings goal?** — not chosen yet · the child picks, you set it
in the app

`key: method.actions.communicate.label`
Communicate

`key: method.actions.communicate.firstTalk`
☐ **Have the first conversation**
*Before the first deposit, not after · wording in section 5*

## 5 · What to say to your child

`key: method.scripts.title`
### What to say to your child

`key: method.scripts.intro` · `text`
Ready-made lines, for moments that really happen.

`key: method.scripts.first.heading`
**The first conversation**

`key: method.scripts.first` · `talk`
🗣️ **What to say**
"From today you have your own money. Every Friday we'll put in ₪30, and it
splits itself into three jars. One for things you feel like right now, one for
a big thing you're saving toward, and one to do something good for someone
else. You decide about each one — I just put the money in."
*Don't explain percentages. Show them the ring.*

`key: method.scripts.ranOut.heading`
**When the money runs out mid-week**

`key: method.scripts.ranOut` · `talk`
🗣️ **What to say**
~~"I told you not to spend it all."~~
"It's gone. That feels bad, and I get it. On Friday there'll be more. Let's
think together about what we'd do differently next time."
*No lecture. The consequence already did the work — your job is just not to
undo it.*

`key: method.scripts.wantsSavings.heading`
**When they want to raid the savings**

`key: method.scripts.wantsSavings` · `talk`
🗣️ **What to say**
"The savings are for the scooter. That's what you decided. I'm not holding it
back for me — I'm holding it for you, for the thing you wanted."
*This isn't a restriction the parent imposes. It's a promise the child made to
themselves, and the parent is just holding it.*

`key: method.scripts.interest.heading`
**When they ask why more coins appear**

`key: method.scripts.interest` · `talk`
🗣️ **What to say**
"Because you left the money in there. Every day it stays, a few more turn up."
*That's the whole explanation. **Don't explain compound interest** — at 7 the
mechanism doesn't land, and explaining turns something that feels like magic
into something that feels like a lesson. The understanding we want comes from
the daily observation, not from the lesson.*

## 6 · What the method doesn't do

`key: method.limits.title`
### What the method doesn't do

`key: method.limits.magnitude` · `text`
**It isn't magic.** A meta-analysis of 37 studies found financial education
improves knowledge substantially, but affects **actual behaviour far less**.
The improvement is real — it just isn't dramatic.

`key: method.limits.allowance` · `text`
**Allowance on its own teaches nothing.** In a large US survey, teenagers who
received allowance with no structure around it scored **lowest** on financial
literacy — lower than those who got no allowance at all. (That's correlation,
not causation.) What teaches is the structure around the money and the
conversation about it — not the money.

`key: method.limits.digital` · `text`
**A screen is not cash.** When people pay by card instead of cash, they're
willing to pay more for the same thing — up to twice as much in one study. In
Israel, 85% of parents still give allowance in cash.

`key: method.limits.recommendation` · `text`
**Our recommendation, despite this being an app:** at this age, hand over the
spending money as **actual cash**, and use the app as the ledger and as the
savings jar.

`key: method.limits.interest` · `text`
**The interest here is the family bank's rate, not a real bank's.** It's set
high on purpose — a realistic rate is simply invisible at the pace of a
7-year-old. Don't present it to your child as what a bank pays.

## Formats

Three block types, visually distinct. See variant E in
`mockups/method-page.html`.

| Type | Looks like | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `text` | Text on the white card | The explanation |
| `quote` | Filled tint, side rail, citation line | A research finding |
| `talk` | Outlined speech bubble with a tail, 🗣️ label | What to say to the child |

**Actions (tickers)** appear only where there is something to do — decide, set
in the app, or communicate to the child. Never on explanatory content.

**Default behaviour:** every section is **closed**. The opener (goal + in
short) is always visible.
