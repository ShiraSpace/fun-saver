🧒 Child mode — mockups

Sunshine Quest theme, 360px phone. Read-only, one screen, no saving goal (that ships as its own feature). Example: ₪23.40 spending · ₪148.90 savings · ₪9.60 good deeds.

Research → rules applied here

  • Whole shekels, rounded down. Decimal money is taught at 8–9, so a 7-year-old sees ₪23, never ₪23.40. Rounding down means the child never sees money they don't have.
  • Counts, not percentages. No donut, no rate, no chart. Growth is shown as a count, "earned by itself +₪6", next to the child's own money (jar-method.md §6.1: show growth, never the mechanism).
  • One screen, no scrolling. NN/g: young children avoid scrolling. Everything fits in 760px.
  • Big targets, icon plus word. Rows are at least 76px (≈2cm, NN/g). Never an icon alone or text alone (Google Designed for Families).
  • Savings leads, spending stays clear. Savings is the hero, as you asked. Spending gets the second biggest number, because it's what a child asks at the shop.
  • Not babyish. Same theme and cards as the parent app, only bigger and with less on screen.

1 · Child home — savings first

Savings is the hero: the biggest number, split into the money the child put in and what it earned by itself since savings began. Spending comes next and stays easy to read at a glance. The saving goal's picture and progress bar will go inside the savings card in the next feature, so the card is built to grow.

2 · The menu

The parent menu has three page tabs, the account picker, and settings. The child menu has a picture of the child, colours to choose, and the way back to parent mode. Switching back is a plain toggle with no gate, as decided.
